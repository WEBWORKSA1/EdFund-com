529 college savings plans, explained

A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged investment account for education. Started early, it can cover a large share of college costs.

How a 529 plan works

You contribute after-tax money, invest it (usually in age-based portfolios), and withdrawals are federally tax-free when used for qualified education expenses. Many states also give a state income tax deduction or credit for contributions. Anyone can open one for any beneficiary, and you can change the beneficiary to another family member.

Qualified expenses

  • College tuition, fees, books, supplies and required equipment
  • Room and board for students enrolled at least half-time
  • Computers and internet access used for school
  • K–12 tuition, up to an annual federal limit
  • Registered apprenticeship costs, and student loan repayment up to a lifetime limit

Non-qualified withdrawals owe income tax plus a 10% penalty on the earnings portion (exceptions apply, e.g. when the student receives a scholarship).

Choosing a plan

  1. Check your home state's plan first. The state tax benefit may make it the best choice.
  2. Compare fees (expense ratios). Lower fees compound into big differences.
  3. Look at investment options and past performance of age-based portfolios.
  4. Prefer direct-sold plans unless you want an advisor.

How much should you save?

Use our 529 savings calculator. A common approach is to aim to cover about one-third of expected costs from savings, with the rest from current income, aid and scholarships.

Impact on financial aid

A parent-owned 529 is reported as a parent asset on the FAFSA, which typically has a small effect on aid eligibility. Rules changed for grandparent-owned 529s in recent FAFSA updates: distributions no longer count as student income on the FAFSA.

529-to-Roth IRA rollover

Under federal law, unused 529 funds can be rolled into the beneficiary's Roth IRA, up to a $35,000 lifetime limit, if the account has been open at least 15 years and annual Roth contribution limits are respected.
