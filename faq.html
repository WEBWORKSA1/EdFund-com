Scholarships & grants

What's the difference between a scholarship and a grant?
Both are free money you don't repay. Grants are usually need-based and come from governments or schools. Scholarships are often merit- or criteria-based and come from many kinds of organizations.
How many scholarships should I apply to?
More than you think. Treat it as a numbers game: aim for 2–4 applications a week during senior year, and reuse and adapt your essays.
How can I spot a scholarship scam?
Red flags: application fees, "guaranteed" wins, requests for bank details up front, and pressure to act immediately. Legitimate scholarships don't charge you to apply.

Loans

Should I take federal or private loans first?
Federal first, in almost every case. They offer fixed rates, income-driven repayment, deferment and forgiveness options. Use private loans only to fill remaining gaps.
Does checking options on EdFund affect my credit?
No. EdFund does not pull credit. Lenders' prequalification uses soft inquiries, and a hard inquiry only occurs if you formally apply.
